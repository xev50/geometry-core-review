Chapter 1: The Pythagorean Theorem

The Pythagorean theorem is a fundamental relation in Euclidean geometry among the three sides of a right triangle. It states that the area of the square whose side is the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the areas of the squares on the other two sides.

a² + b² = c²

This theorem can be written as an equation relating the lengths of the sides a, b and c, often called the Pythagorean equation. By understanding this concept, students can solve for the missing length of any right triangle, provided the other two lengths are known.

Chapter 2: Area and Circumference of a Circle

Understanding the properties of a circle is crucial for advanced geometry. The distance around the boundary of a circle is called the circumference. The space occupied by the circle in a two-dimensional plane is its area.

Area = πr²
Circumference = 2πr

Where 'r' represents the radius of the circle, which is the distance from the center point to any point on the edge of the circle. Pi (π) is a mathematical constant approximately equal to 3.14159.

Chapter 3: Introduction to Trigonometric Ratios

Trigonometry focuses on the relationships between side lengths and angles of triangles. The three primary trigonometric functions are Sine, Cosine, and Tangent. These are frequently abbreviated as sin, cos, and tan.

A helpful mnemonic for remembering these ratios is SOH CAH TOA:

  • Sine: Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • Cosine: Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • Tangent: Opposite / Adjacent

These ratios are essential for solving complex architectural, engineering, and physics problems where direct measurement is impossible.
